# Warehouse Operator — Lore & World

## The Setup
The Knox Event hit Louisville hard. The military quarantine collapsed. WHO ("Warehouse Hazmat Outfitters") — officially a hazmat logistics company, actually a shadow operator network — was stationed at a small warehouse outside Louisville. Their location turned out to be lucky: outside the quarantine zone, intact, defensible.

## The Player
The player is a freshly recruited WHO Operator. Anonymous. Disposable. Replaceable. WHO doesn't invest in Operators who don't survive their first jobs. The progression from "probation" to "trusted asset" is gated through performance: complete missions, prove competence, unlock systems.

## The Map Reality
- **Warehouse (1)**: Player's base. Isolated, defensible, but bare. No food beyond two vending machines.
- **Checkpoint (2)**: Military quarantine point on the main road. Loud, zombie-dense, the official way into Louisville.
- **Rail Yard (3)**: Alternative passage into the city. Quiet, tight quarters, less zombies but harder navigation.
- **Wood Routes**: The long way around. Safest, slowest.

WHO Operators learn the routes. The city is reachable, but only after equipment and trust are established.

## WHO as Organization
- **Front**: Hazmat materials handling, freight logistics
- **Reality**: Operator network managing extraction missions in contaminated zones
- **First handler — COMMAND**: appears to be a janitor in his late 50s (greying mustache, fat cigar, slightly tousled hair). Actually a disguised colonel. Cool A-Team-Hannibal energy.
- **Communication**: Codec-style briefings via the warehouse terminal
- **Logistics infrastructure**: Daily trucks for resupply, ordered through terminal once unlocked

## The Operator Lifestyle (the gameplay loop)
1. **Probation (T1)**: Five small missions in walking distance of the warehouse to prove worth
2. **Establishing**: Q1 reward = food delivery system + supply shop unlock
3. **Equipment**: Q2-Q4 build out base, tools, fortification
4. **Graduation (Q5)**: Final test → Vehicle drop, city missions become possible
5. **Operator Status (T2+)**: Real extraction missions into Louisville and beyond

## Currency: Laundered Garments
Zombies wear clothes. Clothes can be looted, washed, traded. WHO's logistic infrastructure converts garments into supply credits. This makes every zombie kill economically meaningful — not just XP, actual currency. (Implementation: Phase 5d)

## Tone Reference
- 90s action movie energy (Bruce Willis era)
- Self-aware but committed cheesy
- Operator language: terse, professional, dry humor
- Lore-light, gameplay-first — atmosphere through detail, not exposition
